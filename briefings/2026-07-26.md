### Claude Opus 5: Frontier-Adjacent Agentic Model at Half the Cost of Fable 5

Source: Latent.Space (AINews) + Simon Willison's Weblog + Anthropic · Published: 2026-07-24 · Read time: ~8 min

**Elevator pitch:** Anthropic releases Claude Opus 5, an agentic-first model that reaches near-Fable-5 benchmark performance on coding and automation tasks at unchanged Opus pricing, with measurably stronger prompt injection resistance than any prior Claude model.

**Opinion vs. fact:**
- [FACT] Opus 5 is priced at $5/M input and $25/M output tokens — same as Opus 4.8
- [FACT] Batch processing and prompt caching discounts remain (up to 50% and 90% respectively)
- [FACT] Available on Claude Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans; via AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Foundry
- [FACT] Opus 5 scores 43.3% on Frontier-Bench (agentic coding), above both Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol
- [FACT] On FrontierCode v1.1, Opus 5 scores 53.4%, topping the field
- [FACT] On CursorBench 3.2 (in-editor coding assist), Opus 5 lands within 0.5 points of Fable 5 at max effort, at roughly half the cost per task
- [FACT] On Zapier's AutomationBench, Opus 5 completed a full churn-prevention workflow end-to-end at 100%, at ~1.5x the throughput of the next-closest model at equivalent cost
- [FACT] Boris Cherny (Anthropic) stated at launch that Opus 5 is "our least prompt injectable model yet," citing system card findings at page 73
- [FACT] System card (July 24, 2026) reports Opus 5 outperforms prior models on agentic safety — largest gains in prompt injection robustness across coding, computer use, and browser use
- [FACT] Opus 5 scores highest on Anthropic's automated behavioral audit among all tested models, including Sonnet 5 and Opus 4.8
- [OPINION] Simon Willison noted the "buzz is positive" but had not completed hands-on evaluation at time of posting
- [OPINION] The Latent.Space headline — "Fable-level performance at Opus price" — reflects the benchmark picture selectively; advantage over Fable 5 holds on Frontier-Bench but not on all dimensions

**References:** Boris Cherny (Anthropic), Claude Opus 5 System Card (July 24, 2026, page 73), Artificial Analysis leaderboard, Frontier-Bench, FrontierCode v1.1, CursorBench 3.2, Zapier AutomationBench

**Justifications:**
Strongest: The benchmark claims are multi-domain and specific — agentic coding (Frontier-Bench), in-editor assist (CursorBench), and end-to-end automation (AutomationBench at 100%) — not just academic evals. The 1.5x throughput claim on AutomationBench is falsifiable and directly relevant to cost modeling. The prompt injection resistance data comes from the system card with a page reference, allowing verification.

Weakest: All benchmark numbers come from Anthropic or partners (Zapier, Cursor). No independent replication is cited. The Frontier-Bench result (beating Fable 5) is notable but single-benchmark; benchmark methodology for agentic tasks varies considerably, and training data contamination risk is non-zero.

**Core points:**
- Opus 5 is designed for long-running autonomous work: it plans, verifies its own output, and loops until success rather than producing a single-shot answer
- At near-Fable-5 agentic coding performance for half the cost, it shifts the model-tier decision for teams currently paying Fable 5 rates for structured, tool-heavy tasks
- Prompt injection robustness is now a first-class safety criterion — Opus 5 is the hardest-to-inject model in the Claude family, with the biggest gains specifically in the scenarios most relevant to browser and computer-use agents
- Available immediately on all major cloud providers with no pricing increase from Opus 4.8
- The system card reports Opus 5 as the most aligned model Anthropic has tested to date, outperforming Sonnet 5, Opus 4.8, and Mythos 5 on adherence to Claude's constitution

**Impact:** For a solutions architect designing agentic OSS workflows, Opus 5 changes two near-term decisions. First, cost modeling: if you are running Fable 5 for structured, repetitive agentic tasks — configuration generation, multi-step validation, automated fault analysis — Opus 5 is worth an A/B eval against your actual task distribution, not just public benchmarks. The CursorBench and AutomationBench results suggest it holds up for tool-driven, loop-heavy workflows at half the API cost. Second, security posture: the prompt injection robustness improvement matters directly for any agent that consumes untrusted content (raw vendor API responses, ingested network logs, third-party data feeds). "Harder to inject" is not the same as "safe," and Anthropic's internal red-teaming is not a substitute for task-specific adversarial testing on your own data, but the gains are real and the system card gives enough specificity to assess which attack scenarios are most improved.
